# User study protocol

Which error message lets a developer find the cause of a dependency conflict
fastest: pip's, uv's or Crux's? This document fixes the design, the
materials and the analysis **before any participant data exists**. Anything
changed after the pilot is recorded in section 10, with the reason.

## 1. Questions and hypotheses

Pre-registered from the proposal (section 3.6), comparing Crux with pip 26.2.1:

- **H1 (time):** the median time to correctly name the requirement to change is at
  least 30% lower with Crux's message than with pip's.
- **H2 (accuracy):** the share of correct answers is at least 15 percentage points
  higher with Crux's message than with pip's.

Secondary, reported but not tested as hypotheses: the same two comparisons
against uv 0.11.32; accuracy on Q2 (which package the conflict is about);
results split into real and planted conflicts.

## 2. Design

Within-subject. Every participant answers 8 conflicts, each shown with **one** of
three messages (pip, uv or Crux), so each person sees every style 2 or 3
times and is compared with themselves.

- **Assignment:** a Latin square over three groups. Participant *p* is in group
  *p* mod 3, and item *k* (S1 = 0, ..., S8 = 7) is shown in style (*k* + group) mod 3.
  With 18 participants every item is seen 6 times in every style.
- **Order:** shuffled per participant with a fixed seed (`assignments.csv`), so
  no item or style is always first or last.
- **Practice:** before the scored items, one practice conflict
  (`planted/direct/d1/none/s0`) is shown three times, once in each style, with
  the right answer given afterwards. Everyone has seen each format once before it
  counts, so the first scored message of an unfamiliar style is not penalised.
- **Blinding:** messages are not labelled with the tool that produced them. The
  formats are recognisable to someone who knows uv, which we accept and report.

## 3. Materials

Generated by `python -m crux.benchmark.study --participants 18 --out study`:

| file | what |
|---|---|
| `items.json` | the 8 items and the practice item, with every message and the answer keys (for scoring; not shown to participants) |
| `assignments.csv` | participant, group, position, item, style |
| `form.html` | the study itself: one self-contained page, no network needed, no answers in it |

### 3.1 How the items were chosen

By rule, from the benchmark, so the choice cannot follow from which tool does
well on them (`crux/benchmark/study.py`, `select_items`):

- **Real (4):** among the 31 real conflicts that are robust (a conflict among
  releases that exist) and have a unique minimal core: the two whose
  requirements file contains requests outside the conflict (the only two such),
  plus the two with the most requirement statements.
- **Planted (4):** for each depth 3, 4, 5 and 6, the first deep planted instance
  (by id) that has at least one request outside the conflict and a pip 26.2.1
  message.

Deep planted conflicts are included on purpose: they are the case the project
is about (proposal, section 2). The benchmark shows that pip names less of the
conflict as it gets deeper (`benchmarks/results/MESSAGES.md`), so these items
favour Crux and uv over pip. This is stated in the report, and results are
always split into real and planted.

### 3.3 What each style shows

Exactly what the benchmark measured (`crux/benchmark/messages.py`):

- **pip 26.2.1:** its output from the first line starting `ERROR:` to the end.
  Download and progress lines before it are left out. This includes pip's fixed
  advice block and, where pip prints it, its false "no matching distributions
  available" line (a known pip bug, `messages.py`), since that is what developers
  see today.
- **uv 0.11.32:** its whole error output, minus warning lines.
- **Crux:** the rendered explanation, wrapped at 80 columns.

All three are shown in the same dark terminal box, in the same font, under the
same requirements file.

## 4. Participants

- **Target:** 18 (a multiple of 3, so the Latin square is balanced); the proposal's minimum
  is 16, and the risk table's floor is 12. Fewer than 12 complete sessions: the study is
  reported as a pilot only.
- **Who:** classmates who have used pip at least once. Not team members, and not
  anyone who has seen Crux's messages or this project's results.
- **Background** (asked in the form, not used to exclude): how often they use pip, and
  whether they have ever fixed a pip dependency conflict.

## 5. Procedure

1. In person, one participant at a time, on the study laptop with `study/form.html`
   open in a browser (works offline). Quiet place; phone away.
2. The runner gives the next unused participant number (1, 2, 3, ... in order of
   arrival; the number sets the group and order). Keep a paper list of the numbers
   used, with no names.
3. The participant reads the consent text, enters the number, answers two background
   questions, does the practice, then the 8 items. The runner does not help or
   comment during the scored items.
4. At the end, the runner clicks "Download answers" and moves the file into
   `study/responses/` (`crux-study-pNN.json`). The page also keeps a copy in the
   browser's local storage as a backup.
5. About 15 minutes per participant.

## 6. Measures

Per item, in each participant's file:

| field | meaning |
|---|---|
| `q1`, `q1_ms` | the request chosen for "Which of these requirements would you change to fix it?", and the milliseconds from the message appearing to clicking Next |
| `q2`, `q2_ms` | the package chosen for "Which package is the conflict about?", and the milliseconds from answering Q1 to clicking Next (`null` where Q2 is not asked) |

Scoring (against `items.json`):

- Q1 is correct if the chosen request is in the minimal core (`q1_correct`).
- Q2 is correct if the chosen package is one of the packages where the
  requirements collide (`q2_correct`).
- "I can't tell" is scored as incorrect.

## 7. Analysis plan

This is fixed now; the analysis code is task 6 and must follow it.

**Per participant and style:**
- accuracy: the share of Q1 answers that are correct;
- time: the median `q1_ms` over correct Q1 answers. This is "time to correctly name the
  requirement", as in the proposal. If a participant has no correct answer in a style,
  that participant's time for the style is missing.

**Tests:**
- Wilcoxon signed-rank tests on the paired per-participant values: Crux vs pip and
  Crux vs uv, for time and for accuracy. That is 4 tests, with the Holm correction at α = 0.05.
- H1 and H2 are judged on the two Crux vs pip tests, together with their effect sizes:
  - *% faster* = 1 − (median over participants of Crux time) / (median over participants of pip time)
  - *accuracy difference* = mean Crux accuracy − mean pip accuracy, in percentage points.
- A hypothesis is supported only if the test is significant after correction **and** the
  effect reaches its target (30% faster; 15 points). Otherwise, we report what was found.

**Descriptive, not tested:**
- per item and per style: accuracy and median time;
- the same split into real and planted;
- Q2 accuracy by style;
- answers by pip experience.

**Exclusions:**
- A Q1 time above 5 minutes counts as an interruption and is treated as missing.
- An unfinished session keeps its completed items.
- Practice answers are never analysed.
- Every exclusion is counted in the report.

**Power:** with 16 to 18 participants, only large effects can be detected. The report says so,
and gives the effect sizes with the paired differences, not only p-values.

## 8. Threats to validity (for the report)

- The items include deep conflicts chosen by rule, where pip is known to be weak
  (3.1), so the planted half is not a random sample of conflicts.
- Two real items (S3, S4) cannot separate the styles on accuracy (3.2).
- uv's format is recognisable to anyone who uses uv; Crux's is new to everyone, which works
  against Crux, as does the single practice round.
- Participants are students and the conflicts are shown out of context: there is no project, no
  documentation and no way to try a fix. The question is narrower than real debugging.
- 16 to 18 participants (7).

## 9. Ethics checklist

- [ ] Ask course staff whether this needs approval, **before** the pilot. Record the answer and the date here.
- [x] Consent text on the first screen: voluntary, can stop at any time, what is recorded.
- [x] Anonymous: a participant number only; no name, email, IP address or browser details in the data.
- [ ] The paper list of numbers used holds no names; destroy it after the analysis.
- [ ] Response files are kept in `study/responses/` and reported only in aggregate.

## 10. Pilot and changes

**Pilot:** 2 people who are not in the main sample, using participant numbers 1 and 2 on a separate copy of
the form. Afterwards, check:
- the total time is at most about 15 minutes;
- no item is confusing for reasons unrelated to the message (for example, a strange package name);
- the download works on the study laptop.

Pilot answers are not analysed.

**Changes after the pilot:** none yet. Any change is listed here with the reason. Then the materials are
regenerated, and the main study starts at participant 1 again.

## 11. How to run it

```bash
python -m crux.benchmark.crux_messages        # Crux's messages (already committed)
python -m crux.benchmark.study --participants 18 --out study
open study/form.html                          # one session per participant
```

`python -m pytest tests/benchmark/test_study.py` checks that the items follow the rule, every
answer key matches the data, the Latin square is balanced, and the form contains no answers.
